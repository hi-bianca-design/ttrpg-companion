# Artist commissioning

**Status:** Proposed. Costs are rough ranges to plan with, not quotes. This is **not legal advice**: have a lawyer or a standard commission template reviewed before the first contract.

## Why commission

Original art is the point of paid packs: it can't be copied from elsewhere, it is a selling point to an audience that cares who made things, and it is ours to sell if the contract is right.

## Rights, the part that matters most

Before any work starts, a written agreement should cover:

| Term | Why it matters | Options |
|---|---|---|
| **What we may do** | We must be allowed to sell the art inside the app, display it, and use it in marketing | Licence the right to use in-app and in promotion |
| **Exclusive or not** | Exclusive costs more and stops the artist reusing it | Non-exclusive is cheaper; exclusive for hero pieces |
| **Duration and territory** | We don't want the right to disappear in two years | Perpetual, worldwide is simplest |
| **Ownership** | Who owns the copyright | Commonly the artist keeps it and licenses it; a buyout transfers it |
| **Credit** | Artists care and the app has an `artist_credit` field | Credit in the pack row, optionally with a link |
| **Modifications** | Cropping, recolouring, making token versions | Allow reasonable adaptation |
| **AI** | Artist confirms the work is their own, not generated | Written warranty. See [Content policy](content-policy.md) |
| **Kill fee and revisions** | Avoids disputes | Define rounds of revisions and what happens if cancelled |
| **Payment schedule** | Protects both sides | A deposit, then the balance on delivery |

## Payment models

| Model | How it works | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flat fee** | A fixed price per piece | Predictable budgets | We carry all the risk if sales are weak |
| **Lower fee + revenue share** | Smaller fee up front, plus a percentage of that pack's net sales | Aligns incentives, lowers upfront cost | Needs accurate sales reporting; defines "net" in the contract |
| **Revenue share only** | No fee, a larger share of sales | Artists with their own audience | Many professional artists decline; real risk for them |
| **Buyout** | One higher price for all rights | Hero pieces we want to own | Higher cost |

**Rough planning ranges** (a hypothesis, to be replaced with real quotes): portraits and similar illustrations can range from tens to a few hundred dollars each depending on artist, complexity and rights. A pack of ten pieces is therefore a real expense. Get quotes, then fill in the pack's production cost in the [pack record](pack-pipeline.md).

## Finding artists

- Artists whose style fits the Night Vigil look (posterized, printed, limited palette). The same style across a pack matters more than polish.
- Portfolio sites and community marketplaces for TTRPG art; commission boards.
- Ask people who already follow the game. A small, passionate artist with a following is a distribution channel as well as a vendor.

Verify identity and portfolio, ask for a small paid test piece before a large commission.

## Brief template

For each piece:
1. **Purpose** (portrait, token, cover, frame) and where it will appear in the app.
2. **Subject** and mood, with original references only.
3. **Format:** dimensions, file type, transparency, colour mode.
4. **Palette and style notes:** link to the design system; limited palette.
5. **Deliverables and deadline.**
6. **Rights summary** (point to the contract).
7. **Credit line** as it should appear.

## After delivery

- Store the signed agreement and the original files.
- Record the artist's credit and any payout terms in the pack record.
- Pay promptly. Reputation with artists is an asset.
- If a revenue share applies, set up a monthly or quarterly statement. Keep it simple.

## Open questions

- A standard contract template: draft it once and reuse it.
- How artist payouts work across the stores and web checkout (net amounts differ by channel).
- Whether to feature artist profiles in the app. This is probably good for the artists and for sales.
